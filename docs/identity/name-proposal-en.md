## The Manacá-da-Serra: Botanical Foundation

The manacá-da-serra (*Tibouchina mutabilis* (Vell.) Cogn., family Melastomataceae) is a species **endemic to the Brazilian Atlantic Forest** with a singular property documented in scientific literature: its flowers progressively change color — **white → rose-lilac → purple** — with all stages coexisting simultaneously on the same tree.

This phenomenon, described in the literature as *sequential color change* (SCC), was studied by Weiss (1991, *Nature* 354:227–229) as a system of **adaptive dynamic signaling**: older flowers (purple) are kept on the plant even after fertilization because they increase the visual attractiveness of the cluster, guiding pollinators toward the young (white) flowers still available.

```
White flower (young)    →   Rose-lilac (adult)       →   Purple (mature)
  Pre-training          →      Fine-tuning            →      Alignment
  (corpus absorption)   →   (instruction/task)        →   (human preferences)
```

The **simultaneous coexistence** of all three stages on the same tree — the central property of the species — is the most structurally precise analogy available in Brazilian nature for an LLM: multiple capabilities (general language, specialized domain, instruction-following) coexisting in a single model.

**Primary botanical references:**
- Cogniaux, A. (1891). Melastomataceae. *Flora Brasiliensis*, vol. 14, part 3. Lipsiae: F. Fleischer.
- Weiss, M.R. (1991). Floral colour changes as cues for pollinators. *Nature*, 354(6350), 227–229. DOI: 10.1038/354227a0.
- Flora do Brasil 2020. Jardim Botânico do Rio de Janeiro. floradobrasil.jbrj.gov.br.
